Read the German text as an accomplished male audiobook narrator.
Preserve the identity and natural timbre of the reference voice

NARRATION
Use a calm, warm, clear delivery with measured pacing and restrained
expression. Keep German pronunciation precise and natural. Give
sentences complete endings and leave natural pauses between paragraphs.

DIALOGUE
Make speaker changes audible through subtle, consistent differences
in pitch, resonance, rhythm, and intonation. Keep these differences
within the comfortable range of the same male narrator. Avoid
exaggerated acting, artificial accents, and abrupt volume changes.

Holmes: measured, precise, slightly brisk, with controlled intonation.
Watson: warmer, more conversational, with a slightly rounder resonance.
Other characters: use modest, consistent contrasts appropriate to
their words and the surrounding narrative.

FEMALE CHARACTERS
Suggest female dialogue with a modest upward shift in pitch, lighter
resonance, and less chest weight. Keep the delivery clear and fully
voiced, not falsetto, breathy, squeaky, or childlike. Do not make all
women sound alike. Convey authority, warmth, restraint, or agitation
only when supported by the scene. Do not add emotion merely because
the speaker is a woman.

SPEAKER TRANSITIONS
Leave a brief natural pause when the speaker changes. Return to the
neutral narrator delivery for reporting clauses such as "sagte Holmes"
or "fragte sie". Resume the character's delivery for their next words.
When the speaker is unclear, stay close to the neutral narrator voice.

Express questions, surprise, irony, and tension through natural
intonation without overacting. Do not read dialogue dashes as words.
Do not add speaker names, explanations, sound effects, or commentary.
